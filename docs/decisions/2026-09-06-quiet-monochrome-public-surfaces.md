# ADR: The public surfaces adopt a quiet monochrome design language and stop rendering fabricated content

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Date**: 2026-09-06
- **Author**: teetangh
- **PR**: `fix/explore-landing-ui-refresh` → `dev`
- **Supersedes**: PR #1213's direction

## Context

The landing page and the explore surfaces had accumulated sixteen years' worth of visual ideas in a codebase that is a few years old. The landing page alone ran sixteen alternating dark and light bands, each carrying its own animated blob, grid or dot overlay, shimmer text, or gradient border, and every section label was a pill badge rather than a heading. `app/globals.css` had grown to 1466 lines to support this, with utilities like `.metallic-gradient`, `.mesh-gradient-dark`, `.gradient-border` and `.noise-overlay` that existed to serve one section each and were never reused. The effect was a page that looked busy rather than considered, and a stylesheet where nobody could tell which utility was load-bearing and which was one section's abandoned experiment.

Underneath the decoration, several sections were not describing the platform at all. `SUCCESS_STORIES` and the `SatisfiedTestimonial` component rendered three invented reviewers with invented quotes; `UPCOMING_EVENTS` was a hardcoded list dated December 2025 and was still being presented as "upcoming" in September 2026; `TRUST_BADGES` asserted claims the platform had not earned. On the expert profile, `AboutSection` filled every gap in a sparse profile with a templated paragraph — "X is a seasoned … expert", "has extensive experience across multiple industries" — that no expert had written, and every plan detail page printed "An experienced professional dedicated to sharing knowledge and expertise." under an instructor regardless of what their profile actually said. Expert cards printed "Headline: Not specified" as a row rather than omitting the headline, and webinar pages fell back to "Platform: Zoom" even though every session actually runs in the product's own video room. None of this was a rendering bug; it was content nobody had reviewed for truth once the initial mock data was replaced by real records.

A third pressure came from an unmerged sibling. PR #1213 (`fix/ui-overhaul`) proposes a motion-heavy direction for the same surfaces — lenis smooth scroll, a cursor-following spotlight grid, word-by-word headline reveals, tilt cards — but it is 237 commits behind `dev` and still renders the pre-#1490 hardcoded stats that this codebase has since replaced with real per-domain counts. Rebasing it forward would mean re-deriving nearly eight months of intervening work inside a branch built on a design premise this refresh explicitly rejects. The two directions cannot both ship to the same routes, and a decision was needed about which one the codebase moves toward.

## Decision

### 1. The design language is quiet monochrome

Elegance comes from restraint, hierarchy and consistency, not from effects. Every decorative utility that existed to draw attention to itself — `animate-blob`, blurred orb divs, `grid-pattern`/`dot-pattern`/`diagonal-stripes` overlays, `silver-text`, `gradient-text`, `glow`, `chrome-shine`, `spotlight`, `mesh-gradient`, `metallic-*`, `noise-overlay`, a `bg-gradient-to-*` used as a section background, and `backdrop-blur` on a section card — is removed from every page this refresh touched. A section background is now flat: `bg-background` for a light section, `bg-muted/40` for the alternate light tone, `bg-zinc-950` for a dark one. A hero may carry exactly one static highlight, a single radial-gradient div with no animation. Section labels are an uppercase, letter-spaced eyebrow line rather than a `<Badge>` or a Sparkles pill. Headings follow one fluid type scale, cap out at `font-semibold` (never `font-bold` at 2xl and above), and numbers are `tabular-nums`. Every section opens with the same eyebrow-then-heading-then-optional-lede block, left-aligned except in the landing hero and the closing CTA. Cards are a hairline border plus a one-step elevation shadow, never `shadow-lg` or heavier, with a consistent hover state of a slightly darker border, one step more elevation and a `-translate-y-0.5` lift. The full rule set future work must read before touching these routes again lives in `docs/marketing/02-public-surfaces-design-language.md`.

### 2. Content with no data behind it is deleted, not restyled

A section, a component, or a single row that exists to say something no data produced is removed rather than given a new look. This is why `SuccessStoriesSection`, `UpcomingEventsSection`, `TrustBadgesSection`, `SatisfiedTestimonial` and `PlatformFeaturesSection` no longer exist as files, why `AboutSection` renders nothing for a field an expert never filled in instead of a templated sentence, why plan detail pages render an instructor's real headline or nothing at all, why the webinar detail page no longer claims a session happens on "Zoom", and why an expert card omits a headline row entirely rather than printing "Headline: Not specified". A page that has less to say now looks shorter instead of looking padded with claims nobody can stand behind. This rule outlives this PR: any future public-page work that is tempted to fill a gap with placeholder copy should delete the gap instead.

### 3. Motion stays, but as one preset

framer-motion is kept on these surfaces because the owner prefers the feel it gives the page over the bundle savings of removing it (see `docs/decisions` history on this point), but every reveal on every page uses the same preset: fade and rise 16px on scroll into view, once, with one easing curve, and a capped stagger for siblings. There is no scale reveal, no horizontal reveal, no word-by-word text animation, no cursor-tracking effect, and no smooth-scroll library anywhere in scope. This is a deliberate rejection of the direction PR #1213 was exploring for the same routes, not an oversight; §4 covers how the two branches relate.

### 4. Exactly one surface stays anchored dark

The landing hero, the explore listings' masthead, and a plan's title band are dark because they are the top of a page and a dark opening reads as intentional rather than as decoration. Below that opening, the expert profile keeps one further dark surface deliberately: the booking panel, because it is the one element on an otherwise light page the visitor is meant to act on, and a dark card is the clearest way to say "this is the thing to press" without a colour that would break the monochrome rule. The `AudienceSplitSection` at the foot of the landing page keeps the organisation-facing panel dark for the same reason — it is one of two competing calls to action sitting in one row, and the dark treatment is what separates it from the light expert-facing panel beside it rather than an accident of which section happened to keep its old background.

## Consequences

Any future change to `/`, `/explore/experts`, `/explore/experts/[consultantId]`, `/explore/programs`, or the plan detail pages under `/explore/programs/plans/**` must read `docs/marketing/02-public-surfaces-design-language.md` before adding a new visual pattern, and must not reintroduce any utility named in §1. `app/globals.css` was pruned from 1466 to 714 lines as part of this work — 59 dead utilities and 12 orphaned keyframes removed, with the reduced-motion block rewritten to name only what survives — and a utility removed for being decorative should not be re-added for a single call site; it should be composed from the existing Tailwind tokens as the brief requires.

This refresh deliberately did not touch `/enterprise`, `/use-cases`, `/explore/community`, `/explore/enterprise/organisations`, `/(pages)/become-an-expert`, or `components/Footer.tsx`. Several of those surfaces — `EnterpriseSections.tsx`, `UseCaseSections.tsx`, the organisations page, the become-an-expert page and the footer — still use `animate-blob` and the `grid-pattern*` utilities, which is the reason those utilities remain in `app/globals.css` rather than being deleted outright: removing them now would break pages nobody has redesigned yet. Bringing any of these surfaces onto this language is future work and is out of scope here.

On PR #1213: this ADR supersedes its direction rather than continuing it. #1213 is 237 commits behind `dev`, still renders the pre-#1490 hardcoded landing stats, and pursues a motion-heavy language this decision explicitly rejects for these routes. Rebasing it forward to reconcile with this work would cost more than the branch is worth; closing it is the owner's call to make, not something this PR does unilaterally, and it is called out here so the decision is not lost.

On PR #1229 (booking calendar): #1229 edits `ExpertProfileClient`'s data logic, `ConsultationPricingToggle`, and one sticky-class line in `ExpertPricing`. This refresh confined its changes to the JSX layout of the same files, adopted #1229's exact sticky className rather than inventing a competing one, and left both pricing toggle components untouched. The two PRs therefore edit non-overlapping concerns in the same files and should merge cleanly in either order.
